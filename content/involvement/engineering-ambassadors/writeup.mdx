## The Mission

Engineering doesn't happen in a vacuum. It requires funding, recruitment, and public buy-in. As an Engineering Ambassador for the University of South Florida, my role is to act as the liaison between the College of Engineering and external stakeholders—including prospective students, industry partners, and alumni. 

This isn't just public speaking; it is operational logistics and technical translation. I am responsible for organizing large-scale outreach events and taking complex engineering disciplines (from RF systems to structural engineering) and making them accessible and compelling to diverse audiences.

## Operational Impact & Leadership

As the Special Events Coordinator on the Executive Board, my day-to-day responsibilities focus on resource allocation and event execution:

- **Large-Scale Event Logistics:** Led the coordination and execution of major college-wide outreach events, including the Engineering EXPO and SHPE Jr. Noche de Ciencias, managing spatial layouts, timelines, and live troubleshooting.
- **Resource Management & Scheduling:** Directed the deployment of the ambassador team, effectively recruiting, scheduling, and tracking volunteer hours to ensure 100% coverage for college requests.
- **Talent Acquisition:** Interviewed and evaluated prospective engineering students for the Ambassador program, assessing both their technical comprehension and communication soft-skills to build a highly effective cohort.
- **Technical Communication:** Delivered high-impact facility tours and presentations to hundreds of visitors, breaking down USF's active research labs, makerspaces, and engineering curriculum.

## Why This Makes Me a Better Engineer

Hardware engineering requires intense focus and isolation, but hardware *deployment* requires communication, leadership, and influence. 

Serving on the Executive Board for the Ambassadors forced me to develop the operational project management skills that are directly applicable to engineering teams. You learn very quickly how to handle scheduling conflicts, delegate tasks efficiently, and speak confidently about technology you didn't personally build. 

The ability to influence others and inspire the next generation of engineers has been one of the highest-leverage things I’ve done during my time at USF.
